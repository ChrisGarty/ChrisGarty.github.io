These are the habits I want to bring to coaching U13+ girls. Most apply across youth soccer: prepare well, communicate clearly, and give players time to play and think.

## Start the season

- **Do:** Explain expectations, coach roles, and who families should contact.
- **Don’t:** Wait for a problem to explain how the team works.
- **Best practice:** Hold a short player-and-parent meeting; send one written season overview.

## Communicate each week

- **Do:** Share schedules, arrival times, changes, and actions needed.
- **Don’t:** Assume everyone saw a last-minute update.
- **Best practice:** Use a predictable weekly message or post-event updates with a “Coming up” section. Follow up directly on important changes.

## Plan training

- **Do:** Choose one theme, two or three coaching points, and an adjustment for attendance.
- **Don’t:** String together unrelated drills or plan long lines.
- **Best practice:** Build from a recent game observation. Share the plan with assistants and decide what success looks like.

## Coach training

- **Do:** Explain briefly, let players start, and add instructions as needed.
- **Don’t:** Stop every mistake, embarrass players, or use running as punishment.
- **Best practice:** Maximize playing time. Give specific feedback and adjust space, numbers, or rules to help players learn.

## Reflect after training

- **Do:** Check that players leave safely and capture useful observations.
- **Don’t:** Judge the session only by its most popular activity.
- **Best practice:** Ask: What worked? What did players learn? What comes next?

## Prepare for games

- **Do:** Confirm logistics, coach roles, rotations, and one or two familiar team goals.
- **Don’t:** Introduce several new tactical ideas before kickoff.
- **Best practice:** Use a written rotation plan and a simple, active warmup with a ball.

## Coach games

- **Do:** Observe, use brief cues, and give substitutes something to watch for.
- **Don’t:** Direct every pass, argue with officials, or substitute a player simply for making a mistake.
- **Best practice:** Save detailed teaching for breaks. At halftime, reinforce positives and make one or two adjustments.

## Review games

- **Do:** Connect observations to team goals and the next practice.
- **Don’t:** Send an emotional debrief or judge everything by the score.
- **Best practice:** Separate what happened from your explanation of it. Choose one or two priorities; let detailed feedback wait until you are calm.

## Close the season

- **Do:** Thank everyone and recognize progress.
- **Don’t:** Compare players publicly or offer only vague praise.
- **Best practice:** Give each player a strength, an area of growth, and a next focus. Choose one coaching habit to improve yourself.

## What matters to me

My U13+ priorities are to keep players enjoying soccer, help them understand the game, and make new players feel welcome. Long talks and constant instructions leave less room for that. I want training and games to give players repeated chances to make decisions, try again, and recognize their own progress.

These are personal coaching notes, not official club policy. The useful test is whether my habits help players learn and want to keep playing.
