Naming areas of the pitch gives players and coaches a shared language. Here are four useful maps of the same field. Throughout, left and right are from our team's perspective facing the goal we are attacking.

## Two halves

The halfway line divides the pitch into **our own half**, containing the goal we defend, and **the opposition's half**, containing the goal we attack. These labels reverse when we change ends. This is an actual field marking; the tactical divisions below are imaginary coaching references. [IFAB: The field of play](https://www.theifab.com/laws/latest/the-field-of-play/)

## Three horizontal zones: the thirds

Divide the length into three equal sections, with boundaries running across the pitch:

| Third | Location | Typical focus in possession |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Defensive | Nearest our goal | Build attacks and find a way out of pressure. |
| Central / middle | Middle of the pitch | Connect players and progress the ball. |
| Attacking / final | Nearest the opposition's goal | Create and finish chances. |

Every player can contribute in every third. The names describe locations, not areas reserved for defenders, midfielders, and forwards. England Football explains how thirds provide positional reference points; FIFA's playing-through-the-thirds session shows how they can connect a team's build-up. [England Football](https://learn.englandfootball.com/articles-and-resources/coaching/resources/2022/How-to-use-space-in-your-sessions) · [FIFA Training Centre](https://www.fifatrainingcentre.com/en/practice/elite-sessions/in-possession/building_from_the_back_skinner.php)

## Five vertical zones: the lanes

These run lengthwise, from goal line to goal line. From left to right:

| Left wide | Left half-space | Central | Right half-space | Right wide |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Outside lane | Inside-left lane | Middle lane | Inside-right lane | Outside lane |

**Wide lanes** provide width near the touchlines. **Half-spaces**, also called inside channels, sit between those wide lanes and the **central lane**. They offer angles to combine inside, play outside, or pass behind defenders. Coaches' Voice uses the outside edges of the penalty area as references for the wide lanes; exact lane widths vary between diagrams. [Coaches' Voice: The half-spaces](https://learning.coachesvoice.com/cv/half-spaces-football-tactics-explained/)

A half-space extends the full length of the pitch. Receiving there *between the opposition's midfield and defense* describes a particular position within that lane. The lane is fixed; the gap between opponents moves.

Combining five lanes with three thirds makes **15 areas**, not 18. The [Soccer Pitch document on Scribd](https://www.scribd.com/document/936322549/Soccer-Pitch) discusses these different frameworks and their uses for team spacing.

## The 18-zone system

This separate map uses **three columns across the width and six rows along the length**. Each third contains two rows, or six zones. The common numbering is shown below, attacking upward. [Spielverlagerung: Six-by-Three](https://spielverlagerung.com/glossary/pitch-zones/six-by-three/)

**Opposition's goal — attack ↑**

| Left | Center | Right | Third |
| :---: | :---: | :---: | --- |
| 16 | 17 | 18 | Attacking |
| 13 | **14** | 15 | Attacking |
| 10 | 11 | 12 | Middle |
| 7 | 8 | 9 | Middle |
| 4 | 5 | 6 | Defensive |
| 1 | 2 | 3 | Defensive |

**Our goal**

**Zone 14** is the central area immediately outside the opposition's penalty area. A player receiving there may be able to shoot, combine, or pass into the box. It is a useful reference for both creating chances and protecting the middle when defending. It is not another name for a half-space. The Scribd guide highlights this area; the numbering is also illustrated in [Soccer Tactics 2014](https://www.bennionkearny.com/wp-content/uploads/2020/03/Soccer-Tactics-2014-Free-PDF.pdf).

Treat the grid as a schematic: its lines are not official markings, and diagrams may vary in proportions.

## How I use this language

For my U11–U14 players, I would start with halves, thirds, and wide versus central space, then introduce half-spaces when they help explain a real game situation. Memorizing 18 numbers is less useful than recognizing where a teammate can receive.

In my “play to the corners” article, the target is reachable wide space in the attacking third, behind the opposition's defenders. That describes both the location and the opportunity; a zone number alone cannot tell a player whether the pass is on.
